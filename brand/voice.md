# Voice

Express sounds like a recruiter who has done this for twenty years and has no interest in overselling it. Plain, warm, specific, and never breathless. The brand promise is *Respecting People. Impacting Business.*, and the voice has to carry both halves at once: kind about people, sharp about outcomes.

## Tone in a sentence

Talk to one person about their situation, tell them exactly what happens next, and do not dress it up.

## Texture

- **Person.** Second person for the reader ("you", "your business", "your next move"). First person plural for Express ("we", "our team", "your local Express team"). Never third person about yourself.
- **Sentence length.** Short to medium. Long sentences are fine when they carry a list, but two clauses is usually the ceiling.
- **Formality.** Professional, not corporate. Contractions are allowed and common on the franchise and campaign material. The ANZ blog leans more formal and often writes out "that is" and "you are" in full, which reads considered rather than stiff.
- **Humour.** Rare and dry. Never a pun on the brand name and never a joke at a job seeker's expense. Someone reading this may be out of work.
- **Confidence.** State the fact, then stop. Express is the number one staffing franchise; it does not need adjectives to prove it.

## Language rules

- **Australian English** in all ANZ material. Customise, organisation, recognised, specialise, centre, licence (noun) and license (verb), programme only in formal titles, otherwise program.
- **Two exceptions.** Proper nouns keep their registered spelling: *Specialized Recruiting Group*, and any headline lifted verbatim from Express Employment International. Do not "fix" those.
- **Dates DD/MM/YYYY.** The ANZ blog publishes 2/08/2026, not 08/02/2026.
- **Money in AUD or NZD**, stated. Global figures in USD, stated.
- **Local vocabulary.** EOFY, superannuation, labour hire, casual, Fair Work, award rates. Not 401(k), not W-2, not "at-will".
- **Job titles as Express names them.** Office Services, Light Industrial, Skilled Trades, Professional. Capitalised, because they are service lines rather than descriptions.

## Punctuation

- Sentence case for headings on the corporate register. The franchise register sets headings in uppercase, which is a typographic treatment applied to sentence-case copy, not a licence to write in caps.
- **Serial comma is inconsistent in Express's own copy**, and the split is informative. Copy syndicated from Express Employment International uses it ("full-time jobs, part-time positions, and temporary roles"). Copy written in ANZ usually does not ("establish, market and own your franchise"). For new ANZ material, drop it unless it prevents a misreading.
- Express's own copy does use a spaced dash for an aside ("it is work that matters and gives people hope"). Keep it occasional. A full stop is almost always better.
- Exclamation marks appear on Express call-to-action buttons ("Contact a Local Office Today!"). One per page is the limit, and never in body copy.

## Approved phrases

These are load bearing. Reuse them rather than rewriting them.

- Locally owned, global resources.
- It's the best of both worlds.
- Respecting People. Impacting Business.&trade;
- Helping as many people as possible find good jobs by helping as many clients as possible find good people.
- We help people succeed.
- You will never pay a fee for our services.
- Any position, any industry.
- The workforce solutions you need, when you need them.
- Find a job. / Hire people.
- Your job journey.
- Get in the Express lane.
- Change lives, including your own. (franchise material only)
- Build a business helping people find jobs. (franchise material only)
- Ready to back yourself, without going it alone? (Recruit To Own only)

## Banned

- **"Resources", "headcount", "units", "bodies"** for people. Express places associates, not resources. This is the single fastest way to sound off-brand.
- **"Cheap", "budget", "low cost".** Express competes on fit and process, never on price.
- **"Guaranteed"** attached to a placement, an outcome, or an income. Franchise material has legal constraints here; the Recruit To Own salary figure is published and specific, so quote it exactly or not at all.
- **"Unemployed"** as a label for a reader. They are looking for work, or between roles, or job seeking.
- **AI-flavoured filler.** Delve, leverage, elevate, unlock, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, game-changing, in today's fast-paced world, it's not just X, it's Y.
- **Superlatives Express has not earned in writing.** "World's number one global staffing franchise" is fine because Entrepreneur ranked it. "The best recruiter in Sydney" is not.
- **US-specific benefits language** in ANZ material. No medical benefits eligibility, no 401(k), no CDL.

## Two rewrites, for calibration

**Off brand.** "Express Employment Professionals leverages a best-in-class, technology-enabled talent acquisition framework to seamlessly connect world-class candidates with forward-thinking organisations."

**On brand.** "One connection with Express gives you access to multiple job opportunities. Our local team knows the employers who are hiring, and we will match you to the job that fits your skills and your situation."

**Off brand.** "Are you an experienced recruiter ready to unlock your entrepreneurial potential and embark on a transformative journey to business ownership?"

**On brand.** "You've got the experience. We've got the brand, backing, and salary to help you go out on your own, with less risk and more reward."

## Register

Express writes in two registers, and they do not mix on one page. See [visual-system.md](./visual-system.md) for the visual half of the same split.

- **Corporate.** Job seekers and employers. Sentence case, measured, service first. The default.
- **Franchise.** Prospective Franchise Owners. Uppercase headlines, shorter sentences, direct challenge ("Tired of building someone else's business?"), investment language. Louder, but never hyped.

A job seeker should never be sold a franchise, and a franchise prospect should never be handed a job board.
